Heuristics cheat sheet

The decisions an interviewer will ask you to defend, on one page. Mostly from ch. 1, 4, and 10.

1 · Classify the subdomain

CoreGenericSupporting
Competitive advantageYesNoNo
ComplexityHighHigh (but solved)Low
VolatilityHighLowLow
ImplementationIn-house, best peopleBuy / adopt open sourceIn-house cheaply or outsource

Quick test: Could we buy it? → generic. Would doing it better win customers? → core. Otherwise → supporting.

2 · Choose the integration pattern

SituationPattern
Teams communicate well and succeed or fail togetherPartnership
A small piece of model genuinely must be sharedShared kernel
Upstream won't adapt and its model is fine for youConformist
Upstream model is messy or volatile, or you're coreAnticorruption layer
Upstream has many consumers and wants to evolve freelyOpen-host service (+ published language)
Integration costs more than duplicationSeparate ways

3 · Decision tree: logic → architecture → tests

If…Business logicArchitectureTesting
Core + money / audit / deep analysis neededEvent-sourced domain modelCQRSPyramid
Complex business logicDomain modelPorts & adaptersPyramid
Simple logic, complex data structuresActive recordLayered (4 layers, with service layer)Diamond
Simple logic, simple dataTransaction scriptLayered (3 layers)Reversed pyramid

Choose the logic pattern first. The architecture and testing strategy follow from it. Re-run the tree when a subdomain changes type (ch. 11).

4 · Boundary sizing

  • Bounded contexts: volatile or new core subdomain → start wide and split later (ch. 10).
  • Aggregates: as small as strong consistency allows. Reference other aggregates by ID (ch. 6).
  • Microservices: bounded contexts are the widest valid boundary and aggregates the narrowest. Subdomains are the safe middle that tends to give deep services (ch. 14).

5 · Reliable messaging

NeedPattern

Multi-step process needing central state or branchingProcess manager
Consumer must work while producer is downEvent-carried state transfer
